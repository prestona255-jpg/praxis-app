What Praxis is

Knowledge made with you.

Most reading apps file what you read. Praxis is built for what reading does to you — the note that argues back, the thread between two books that only you can see, the conviction that hardens into theory. Here your marginalia are not exhaust; they are the raw material of your own thought, gathered, connected, and eventually walked by others.

Readers have always worked this way — Praxis is the next station. Tap one.

The whole app is one pipeline: you read, you mark, the margin becomes a note, notes gather into sub-theories, sub-theories thread into an arc, and an arc — when it is ready — steps into the commons to be walked. Every surface exists to move thought one station down that line. Nothing here is decoration.

Critical pedagogy

You are not an empty account.

Education is never neutral. How we learn — and who gets to make meaning — is always a question of power.

It runs through Paulo Freire, bell hooks, and Audre Lorde, among others. Freire named the "banking" model — where a teacher deposits facts into a student treated as an empty account — and set against it a problem-posing education, where teacher and learner make knowledge together, beginning from the learner's own world. hooks insisted this could be done with love, as a practice of freedom. Lorde reminded us that the master's tools will never dismantle the master's house.

Praxis is built in that lineage.

The banking model — knowledge flows one way: the teacher deposits, the student receives.

What it refuses to become

No counts that flatter.

no·1No feed. Home is a desk, not a stream. Nothing scrolls forever.
no·2No likes, no ranks. Thought travels by consequence — walked, questioned, built on — never by applause.
no·3No harvesting. Your reading is not a product. What the app knows, you can read; what it remembers, you granted.
no·4No ghostwriting. Yumi asks more than she answers. The theory that leaves this place is yours.

The lexicon

The words this place uses.

praxis
Reflection and action, together — Freire's word for reading that intends to change something. Not study for its own sake; study that returns to the world.
mark
The symbol a sub-theory wears — its face in the field. Chosen once from the shape-and-color vocabulary, then kept for life, so a thought stays recognizable as it grows.
arc
A body of thought under construction — the sub-theories it holds, the threads between them, and the books that feed it. An arc is where reading becomes a position.
sub-theory
One claim you are building — small enough to test against a page, real enough to defend in public. Arcs are made of these.
thread
A drawn connection between two sub-theories — the visible line of reasoning. Threads carry the flow of an arc; they are how an argument holds together.
register
What kind of note a note is. Marginalia sits beside the text and argues with it; journal sits beside your life and remembers why the text mattered. Same margin, different work.
lens
An idea your whole shelf regroups around — care, power, method — so the same books answer a different question. A lens changes what your library is for without changing what it holds.
maturity
How far a sub-theory has come — shown as glow in the field, never as a percentage. Thought ripens; it doesn't fill a progress bar.
concentrate
Tapping one mark in the field to bring it forward — its threads light, the rest dims. Attention as a gesture.
walk
Reading another person's arc the way it was built — thread by thread, in their order, at their pace. To walk an arc is to think alongside its author.
build-on
What you make in answer to someone's arc — an extension, an objection, a question that sticks. Build-ons travel back to the author: consequence, not applause.
the commons
Where published arcs live — walked, questioned, and built on by other readers. Thought moves here by what it sets in motion, never by counts.
the field
An arc's interior — every sub-theory lit as its mark, arranged by your hand, threads carrying the flow. The shape is yours; Praxis never tidies uninvited.
the portrait
Your account page — an instrument, not a mirror. Where your reading has been and what it keeps reaching for, drawn so you can ask it questions.
Yumi · 由美
"Reasoned beauty." A second mind, on request — she reads along only where you allow it, asks more than she answers, and never writes your thoughts for you. Not a tooltip, not a ghostwriter.
