If Sub-theory 1 argues that desire is a refusal and Sub-theory 2 that eros belongs in the room, this one asks a smaller, more practical question: can wanting itself be taught? Pedagogy of the Oppressed is exact about the failure mode — the banking model deposits facts into a student assumed to want nothing except to be filled. Its quiet violence is not that it under-informs; it under-desires.

Yearning: Race, Gender, and Cultural Politics names the alternative directly: a classroom that treats a student's actual longing — not the school's approved version of ambition — as the beginning of the lesson, not a distraction from it. A note from Hidden Potential says it plainer than either of them: scaffolding is not talent, it is what lets talent survive long enough to become skill. hooks's homeplace is the reason a student would trust the scaffold enough to climb it.

I learned this first, not from a book, but from that first year at Miami Northwestern — a student whose stated goal, "get a job, any job," was not a small ambition but a well-rehearsed refusal to want more where wanting more had only ever cost her. Teaching her had almost nothing to do with content and everything to do with rebuilding, one small provable success at a time, her right to want something specific.
