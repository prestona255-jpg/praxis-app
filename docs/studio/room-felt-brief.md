### RD-2 — THE KEYHOLE = THE LENGTHENING PAGE (kills INT-5 by construction)
The field's ontology: bounded width, growing height, one scroll world.
- The field NEVER exceeds its column width. No horizontal overflow, no horizontal
  scrollbar, no pan — ever.
- Height is a passive consequence of content: lowest card + one card-height of breathing
  room, over a generous minimum. Relaxes on release (no ratchet).
- NO inner scroll context exists on any axis. The page scrolls; the field rides it, like
  the prose column beside it.
- **Frame-flattening rider:** the field stops being pane-in-panel-in-page. It IS the right
  column's ground, edged in CARVED, headed by its own line (see RD-5 for the header text).
- **RD-2a (session refinement, at rec):** the boundary felt law — *you can always place a
  card below the lowest card without entering a mode.* Dragging toward the bottom edge
  extends the ground live under the hand. Mechanism (auto-scroll rate, growth timing) =
  CC session's to build; the sentence is the law it builds to.
- **INT-5 residue:** both field scrollbars cease to exist rather than get styled. The only
  remaining question — thin-styling the PAGE scrollbar — is an app-wide desktop-canon
  question, logged there, not Room scope.

### RD-3 — THE SPARSE FIELD = SETTLED GROUND
- **Spawn law:** new cards settle row-major from top-left into the first open patch
  (left→right, then down), with small organic jitter so the result never reads as a grid.
- **Two absolute laws:** a spawn never overlaps an existing card; a spawn never moves one.
  The user's arrangement is sacred — cards move only under the user's hand.
- **Tripwire clarification (binding):** RD-3's position reads are collision-avoidance —
  layout mechanics, not interpretation. `evidenceLayout` never enters
  `assembleContextData`; T11 stays 0. The build must not confuse reading-for-geometry
  with reading-for-meaning.
- **Empty state:** one `--ink-3` line on the open carve (register: "Notes you gather will
  land here" — final words at build). NO furniture in the void, ever — no dashed hints,
  no ghost cards, no watermark. The carve and the material do the work. Modest empty
  minimum height (a bare workshop spends no scroll on void).
- **Clip law (rides all surfaces):** a note is never cut by geometry. Drag clamps cards
  fully inside bounds; anywhere notes render in a list, truncation is typographic only
  (line-clamp + ellipsis — R6 clamp-line residual's territory), never a geometric crop.

## §3 FELT CANON — the acceptance sentences (Law-8 style)
*These sentences ARE the build's acceptance criteria. A break on any of them returns to
chat — it is not the build session's to reinterpret.*

1. The field reads as ground you own, never as a window onto somewhere else — nothing
   exists off-frame horizontally, and no inner scrollbar exists on any axis.
2. You can always place a card below the lowest card without entering a mode — the ground
   lengthens under your hand.
3. The workshop is one desk — one title, one sheet, one field — and the same note never
   appears on two surfaces at once.
4. Every card sits fully on the ground; no note is ever cut by an edge, a panel, or a pane.
5. New cards settle onto open ground without touching what you've placed — your
   arrangement never moves unless your hand moves it.
6. An empty field is quiet ground with one line of ink; no furniture stands in the void.
7. A short field beside a long sheet is CORRECT — the desk holds what it holds; the field's
   height reflects its contents and is never padded to look fuller.
8. The name is the only large thing on the page, and it asks nothing — the invitation
   lives in the placeholder's ink and Yumi's single line, never in scale.
9. Acts read by kind at a glance: status breathes, modes toggle, doors face each other
   top-right in destination-named pills, and Finish sleeps until the name exists.
10. Nothing floats bare over content — every layered element carries its own ground, and
    the corner holds only the orb.

*(RD-1's sentence rides its own slice, not ROOM-3: "The arc field is daylight — the same
parchment world as the shelf and the workshop, carved, not cut.")*
