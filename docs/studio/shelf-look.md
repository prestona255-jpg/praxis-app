# The Shelf — the finished look (north star)

*A one-page prose picture of what the Shelf should FEEL like when it is done. This is a
synthesis, not a spec: it cites `docs/studio/r-shelf-brief.md` v3 ("THE FELT DIAL", `36b7d45`)
and the July-21 rulings (THE WALL · order-by-life · the desk line · dot-only · §3-governs). Where
a number or law is binding, the brief is the source — this page never overrides it. Read the
brief for the law; read this for the picture.*

---

## The horizon

You arrive at a **window onto a wheat field**. A slim strip runs across the top of the sheet —
two layers of stalks, the near ones taller and warmer, the far ones shorter and a tone dimmer,
all leaning a degree or two in a slow breeze (never faster than an 8-second cycle, never a
gust — brief §3.2). Full stalks with their **heads** sit inside the strip; nothing is clipped.
It is 64px tall on a phone, ~104px on a desktop (the midpoint of the ruled breathe range). It is
**not** a banner and **not** sticky: as you scroll down into the library, the field scrolls away
above you — it is the horizon you leave as you descend (§3.5). Turn motion off and the field
simply goes still; the meaning holds. *The field is where your thinking grows, always on the
horizon of your reading life* (F9).

## The doorway

Below the horizon, a **quiet header**: the wordmark "Shelf," a recessed search, and the one
instrument that changes everything — the **Categories ⇄ Lenses** toggle. Flipping it is not a
filter; it is a **re-shelving act** — the whole case rearranges around a different way of seeing,
the books animating between cavities while the case itself never moves (F1, §5 rider). Manage
folds the maintenance tools away; Add-a-book waits at the corner. On a phone the header, like the
field, scrolls away — the bands themselves are the navigation, and only the Add-a-book button and
Yumi's orb persist (§3.8).

## The desk

In front of the case, on the page's own light ground, is the **desk** — what you are *carrying*
right now. A few books lie in hand, larger than the shelved ones, each on the desk's baseline;
under the one you are actively questioning sits its **carrying-question** in a quiet gold italic.
The desk has no frame and no border — light simply falls on what's in hand (§3.4). When you are
carrying nothing, the furniture disappears and a single line stands in its place: *Nothing in
hand right now.* The grammar is one sentence: **inside the case = shelved; on the desk = in
motion.**

## The case, and the wall

Then the **bookcase**. Each band is a **carved cavity** — one soft inset shadow from the top edge,
a ground one step darker than the page, a real board at the foot — and the books **stand on drawn
shelf lines**, feet on the line, tops breathing with a barely-there ≤6% variance. No book floats;
no caption pushes its neighbor out of true (the caption lives in a fixed zone below each cover).

On a **phone** the case is a single column: each band shows about two shelves' worth of books,
then a **See-all** tile opens that band as its own full view — you have stepped inside the case,
and the horizon is behind you (§3.7).

On a **desktop** the case becomes **THE WALL** (ruled July 21): the bands stand **side by side as
vertical stacks** — two columns on a laptop, three on a wide screen, four on the widest — and you
read your library *across*. Every band shows **all** its books in wrapped rows; there is no
sideways scrolling and no See-all — it is all simply there. Bands are masonry-filled into the
shortest column so the wall stays roughly level, and a band never breaks across a column. The desk
and the wheat span the full width above the wall; the warm sheet-ground runs out past the content
to the edges of the twilight world.

## Order by life

The library is not sorted alphabetically or by taxonomy — it is sorted by **attention** (ruled
July 21). The books you have touched most recently sit nearest the front of each shelf; the bands
whose books are most alive rise to the top of the reading order (and, on the wall, fill first,
across). Nothing announces this — no dates, no badges, no "recent" label. The shelf just quietly
keeps what you are living with closest to hand. *(In the mockup the "touch" is seeded; in the
build it will ride a real signal — marks, status, opens — decided at build time.)*

## The marks

The covers wear their life **quietly** (F3). A single **coal** — one ember, never a cluster —
sits at the corner of a book that carries value-marks, brighter if the marks run deep, and it
**flares to gold** when you tap a matching value chip in the header. A soft **under-glow** rides
real annotation density; an unmarked book has **no halo at all** (g0 = absence — sparse-honest). A
thin **register tick** and an **arc thread** run down the spine-edge; tap the thread and the book's
arcs unfold. Status is a **single dot** — reading, will-read, finished — never a word. The count of
things lives in the chips and in the tap, never shouted from the resting shelf.

## The motion, and the light

Illumination is **one grammar**: search and value-chips **light** what matches and **dim** the
rest (cover opacity only — the cavity ground never crushes darker under a filter, §Law-1 rider).
Illumination never rearranges or hides; only the mode toggle regroups. Ambient motion lives in
the horizon band **and nowhere else**; reduced-motion renders a still field, no substitute breath
(Law 7). The whole surface reads in the **ground spectrum**: you *work* in the light (the shelf, the
desk) and the field warms the horizon above — the deep-amber rooms are elsewhere (CLAUDE.md §7).

## The open dials (what the felt pass decides)

A few things are set by hand and wait for Preston's eye, not for a rule:
- the **breath** of the cover variance (currently a subtle ≤6%);
- the exact **height of the horizon** on desktop (currently the 104px midpoint);
- how **level** the masonry wall should read (life-order binds the fill, so some unevenness is
  structural);
- whether the **desk wants a visible line** or should stay pure bottom-alignment on light;
- the **rest-brightness of the coal** against the carved cavity (the recon-8 dial).

Everything above is floors and pictures. The Shelf is *done* when Preston walks it — phone in one
hand, desktop in front of him — and it feels like his library being **worked**, not just stored.
